# DuneCity campaign

Stefan, 2026-11-19: "build a dune city campaign ... maps should be about defeating enemies,
reaching a certain number of credits or growing to a certain population size, reducing crime and
increasing property values etc. as well as things like there is a breach in the shield wall ...
play test the scenarios so that the support ai can win against an enemy ai one level lower than
itself ... the maps should get harder so the win rate falls a bit even with the difficulty
difference ... aim for 10 maps, including a version of all against atreides which allows for city
building and has room for it for the enemy too."

Ten Atreides missions, `data/maps/singleplayer/DCITY_M01.INI` .. `DCITY_M10.INI`, generated by
`scripts/gen_city_campaign.py` from the city-scenario toolkit (`scripts/gen_city_scenarios.py`).
Missions follow each other in order; a lost mission is replayed.

## Playing it: the DuneCity Campaign menu

Stefan, 2026-11-21: "remove continue from the menu, and add in dunecity campaign as a menu item,
it has the same selection option approach but it doesn't have mods, also before each map you
should have the mentat screen giving you an overview of the map objectives with the proceed
screen. don't need a map screen though. when you win you get the dune planet turning colour
thing."

- **Main menu.** *DuneCity Campaign* sits under *Campaign*, where *Continue* was (Continue and
  its newest-save lookup are gone; *Load Game* still lists every save).
- **Selection screen** (`HouseChoiceMenu` with `duneCity`): the campaign screen without the
  Offline/Online, Maps and Campaign mod choices. Atreides only, offline only; full campaign or a
  single mission, *Start from* mission 1-10, DuneCity skin, AI partner, enemy AI, Game Rules,
  Load Save. Where the mod choice was, the start mission's name and goals are shown, with the
  first-attack minute for the chosen enemy AI. The menu switches to the `dunecity` mod
  (`DuneCityCampaign::kMod`) and leaves it active, as Civilization Wars does with its mod: the
  QuantBot city tiers and the `[DIFFICULTY]` strengths were tuned on it. Without that mod or the
  ten maps the screen says so and offers no start.
- **Before each mission** the Mentat reads the `[BRIEFING]` text sentence by sentence, then shows
  the mission's name and goals (`MissionObjectivePolicy::briefingLines`) beside *Proceed* and
  *Repeat*, e.g. "Mission 1: Sihaya Basin / Goal: 20,000 people, held for 1 minute. / Or destroy
  every enemy house. / Enemy attacks can begin at minute 2."
- **After each win** (debriefing, then statistics) the planet screen plays with no region to
  choose (`MapChoice::DuneCityConquest`): the planet fades in and turns into the region map, the
  regions of the missions before stand in Atreides colours, and this mission's share blends in.
  The art's "Select your next conquest" title gives way to "Mission N of 10 won"; the ticker names
  the mission won and the next one.
  The 27 regions are taken in the original Atreides conquest order (each `REGIONA.INI` group in
  turn, Atreides' own new regions first), 2-3 per mission (`DuneCityCampaign::conquestRange`), so
  after mission 10 the whole planet is Atreides. A click speeds it up; once coloured, a click or six
  seconds continue to the next briefing.

## Mission goals: `[OBJECTIVES]`

An optional section of any scenario INI (`include/MissionObjectives.h`):

| Key | Goal |
| --- | --- |
| `Population=N` | displayed city population of the player's house (internal population x 20) at least N |
| `Credits=N` | the treasury the sidebar shows (`House::getCredits`) at least N |
| `MaxCrime=N` | average crime at the house's developed buildings at most N (0-255; Safe is below 64) |
| `MinLandValue=N` | average land value at least N (0-250; Middle Class from 80) |
| `MaxPollution=N` | average pollution at most N |
| `DefeatEnemies=1` | every enemy house must also be gone |
| `HoldSeconds=N` | every goal must hold together for N seconds (default 30) |

All listed goals must hold at the same time for `HoldSeconds`. Crime, land value and pollution
goals never pass on a city without developed buildings. The goals add to `WinFlags`: a map that
keeps `WinFlags` bit 1 is still won by destroying every enemy; `WinFlags=2` with
`DefeatEnemies=1` requires the goals and the conquest. A deadline is the existing `TimeOut` with
`WinFlags` bit 8 and no `LoseFlags` bit 8 (time out = mission failed); with `LoseFlags` bit 8 the
time-out is a survival victory. `WinFlags` bit 0x10 stays meaningless (protocol 85).

The game evaluates the goals every 32 cycles after the city phase, announces the summary and each
reached goal in the news ticker, and shows an **Objectives** panel at the top right of the map
with each goal's current value, the hold countdown and any time limit. On DuneCity campaign
missions the panel is shown even without city goals: it counts the enemy houses still standing
when destroying them wins, and shows the hold-out or deadline clock. The goals and their hold
state are saved (`SAVEGAMEVERSION` 9887). Protocol 130.

## Timed events: `[EVENTS]`

`BreachN=minute,x,y,width,height[,worms]` opens a mountain wall: at that game minute every
mountain tile in the rectangle becomes sand, the sandworm regions are rebuilt so worms can cross,
up to `worms` (0-8) new Natives sandworms surface in the gap, and the news ticker shows
`BreachNMessage` (default "The Shield Wall has been breached!"). The breach is a saved trigger
(`BreachTrigger`, trigger type 3).

`FirstEnemyAttack=N` is a campaign mission's opening grace period: no QuantBot house hostile to
the objective house launches an attack wave before game minute N (they still build, defend and
fight what comes to them). It is saved with the objectives.

## Difficulty: `[DIFFICULTY]`

`Easy=`, `Medium=`, `Hard=`, `Brutal=`: how strong the enemy houses are for the enemy AI's
difficulty (the campaign menu's enemy AI; QuantBot labels). When the mission starts, every enemy
house's starting credits are multiplied by the value of its AI's difficulty (rounded to 100) and
the `FirstEnemyAttack` grace period is divided by the strongest enemy's value
(`MissionObjectivePolicy::enemyStrengthPermille`, `INIMapLoader`). A missing key or an AI without a
difficulty label plays the map as authored (1.0). The generator writes each mission's values
(`Mission.difficulty` in `scripts/gen_city_campaign.py`).

## Low spice

Stefan, 2026-11-19: "these campaign maps ... should have low spice. it is supposed to be about
building a city". The generator turns thick spice into ordinary spice, removes spice blooms and
erodes every field from its edge until a quarter of the layout's spice tiles remain
(`thin_spice`, `Mission.spice_keep`): 2-5% of each map instead of 6-19%. Field positions stay;
the largest fields keep their cores and the smallest vanish, so taxes carry the economy.

Cities need rock (Stefan: "remember we need rock for building cities"): every rock shelf then grows
ring by ring into the open sand beside it, never onto spice or mountains, until at least 30% of the
map is rock (`grow_rock`, `Mission.rock_floor`; Hagal Flats was 15%, Harg Pass 17%, Cielago 19%).
Shield Wall Breach grows only inside its basin (to 75% of the basin floor), so the flats outside
keep the sand its breach worms cross.

## Briefings: `[BRIEFING]`

`Name=` is the mission's name (menu, briefing and planet screen). `Text=`, `Win=` and `Lose=` are
the Mentat's briefing and debriefing for a DuneCity campaign mission (`BriefingMenu`, with the
mission's map file name). Other campaigns keep the TEXT files. The goals shown after the briefing
come from the loaded mission (`MissionObjectives::briefing`), so its first-attack minute is the
one the enemy strength set.

## Campaign rules

`misc/DuneCityCampaign.h`. A game is in the campaign when its scenario file name is
`DCITY_M??.INI`, so saves, retries and the next mission carry it with no other state. Unlike the
original campaign:

- the INI `TechLevel` applies (not the mission number);
- `Brain=TeamN` rivalries are kept (the campaign roster puts every computer house on team 2);
- every QuantBot plays Custom rules: no original-campaign policy (`campaignPolicyApplies`) and no
  Campaign game mode (delayed first attack, army capped at the starting value);
- no Meanwhile or Finale cut-scenes and no region choice (the planet screen only colours the
  regions won); online co-op is not offered yet.

## QuantBot and the goals

`[Force Concentration] CityObjectivePlanning` (hashed, default 1; overlay
`tests/ai/play-styles/city-objective-planning-off.ini` for the OFF arm). The QuantBot that
serves the objective house:

- treasury goal: orders Silos until storage plus starting credits can hold the goal, and keeps a
  bank floor of half of all income earned since the start (refined spice plus taxes), so the
  starting cash stays free for the build-up while the treasury rises with the economy; once it
  has added half of what the goal asks beyond the mission's starting treasury (recorded at the
  first evaluation and saved) it spends only the surplus above the goal. A military emergency
  releases the bank. Counting the starting cash as progress froze Tuono's helper from minute 0
  (v3); spending freely until halfway left Brutal and Hard helpers on a few thousand credits for
  45 minutes (v5);
- population goal: its Custom city ceiling is at least 125% of the goal;
- crime or land value goal: police services (first station, catchment placement, land value
  budget) from the first developed block, with police funded at 100% while it has working cash.
- a treasury goal never starves the defence: the hold releases while the house defends its
  economy, while an enemy ground force threatens one of its assets, or while its ground army is
  below half of the strongest enemy's estimated army (`objective_treasury_release`). It relied on
  `militaryEmergency`, which is computed only while a house saves for technology, so on low-spice
  Tuono the helper banked 13,000-19,800 credits while its army fell to nothing.

### City growth and land value on low spice

Stefan: "the ai needs to be tuned to know how to grow a city to boost economy when there is low
spice" and "the AI needs to learn how to boost property prices. for example palaces boost nearby
property values which is good for tax". On the low-spice maps spice runs out by minute 10-15 and
taxes carry the economy: a Hard helper on Harg Pass grows from 13,000 people to 100,000-133,000
by minute 30-40, while enemies that keep idle harvesters stall (a Hard Harkonnen at 14,000).

- **Land value** (`[Force Concentration] CityLandValueSiting`, default on, hashed when off; overlay
  `city-land-value-siting-off`). A Palace or Stadium lifts land value by 40 within 8 tiles
  (`CityEffects.h`). Their sites are now scored by the land-value falloff they put on the house's
  own residential and commercial lots, and `palace_land_value` buys a palace (within the palace
  target) once a dozen such lots stand and its price plus 1,000 is in hand, instead of after 5,000
  spare credits and both factories. Paired on 76 low-spice games (Hard helper v Hard, eight seeds,
  all missions): tax receipts +8% at minute 20 and +6% at minute 25, population +6%, wins 61 v 62.
- Existing city-economy switches did not help here (helper-only, 80 paired games each): spice
  transition and single-yard growth changed nothing, power-growth horizon +1 win, spice-capacity
  runway cut population at minute 15 from 25,500 to 17,000.

### The tiers in the campaign: the skill ladder

The campaign has to work two ways at once. Stefan, 2026-11-19: a helper beats enemies one tier
lower; and "the player support ai should lose at least 60% of the time or the map or ai needs
tuning for medium vs medium, hard - hard, brutal - brutal". A map strength can only satisfy both when
the helper one tier up plays clearly better than a same-tier helper. On the low-spice maps no tier
reaches its army or city ceiling, so the tiers played the same game: on the same seeds and maps a
Brutal helper won 106 of 200 games against Hard enemies where a Hard helper won 100.
Stefan, 2026-11-20: "Yes, build it" (give the tiers distinct skills).

`include/players/QuantBotCityCampaignSkills.h`, `[Force Concentration] CityCampaignSkillLadder`
(default on, hashed when off; overlay `city-campaign-skill-ladder-off`). It applies only in DuneCity
campaign missions, to helpers and enemies alike; everywhere else every QuantBot keeps its native
behaviour.

**Thinking speed** separates the tiers. A tier decides on one AI pass in N (`CityCampaignThink<Tier>`,
1-16; an AI pass is 50 cycles, about 0.8 s): Easy 8, Medium 6, Hard 3, Brutal 1. A slower tier zones,
orders production and commands its units less often; its income and ceilings are the same. A Hard
helper deciding on every 2nd, 3rd, 4th or 6th pass won 17, 12, 4 and 5 of 80 games against Hard
enemies where every pass won 24 (`hs-think-hard-*`). With 8/6/3/1 on eight seeds at the earlier
strengths (`tl-8631`, 80 games per pairing):

| Pairing | Medium v Easy | Medium v Medium | Hard v Medium | Hard v Hard | Brutal v Hard | Brutal v Brutal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Helper wins | 67 | 36 | 59 | 22 | 40 | 21 |

The helper one tier up now wins 23 (Hard v Medium) and 18 (Brutal v Hard) more games than a
same-tier helper against the same enemies; before the ladder the gap was 0-6.

**Skills** (`CityCampaignSkill<Name>`, the lowest tier that plays each; 4 = no tier): small
decisions the AI already made, kept from the lower tiers.

| Skill | Tier | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| TreasuryRelease | Medium | a house saving for a credits goal spends the bank when its base is threatened |
| CityServices | Medium | a crime or land-value goal gets police beside the homes and the land-value police budget |
| LandValueSiting | Hard | a Palace or Stadium is sited among the house's own homes and shops |
| PalaceForTax | Hard | a Palace is bought as a land-value investment once 12 homes and shops stand |
| FactoriesFirst | Hard | a funded factory backlog takes a construction yard before zoning does |
| EarlyAttack | Brutal | attacks at `CityCampaignBrutalAttackPercent` (15%) of its army ceiling, not 25% |
| GrowthTax | none | 3% tax while the city is small (screened, no gain) |
| HarvesterTargets | none | ground combat shoots harvesters first (screened, no gain) |

FactoriesFirst is `[Force Concentration] ProductionBeforeCityGrowth` (default on), which every
other city game plays: a traced mission 6 game showed a Brutal helper keep all four construction
yards on city growth for eight minutes while it reported a funded heavy-unit backlog, banked
13,000-22,000 credits and lost its army. EarlyAttack's 15% (`CityCampaignBrutalAttackPercent`,
0 = the tier's 25%; overlay `city-campaign-brutal-attack-native`) won 57 of 80 games for a Brutal
helper against Hard enemies where 25% won 43.

The skills alone moved a Hard helper's wins by at most a few of 80 (`ladder-v1`: Brutal v Hard 21,
Hard v Hard 24); taking EarlyAttack from Brutal cost 6 of 240 (`ea-never` 116 v `ea-ctl` 122).

Tried and dropped, measured on the same seeds:

- a steeper thinking ladder, 12/8/4/1, fitted worse across the ten missions (total shortfall 0.59
  against 0.44 for 8/6/3/1, `grid-12841`);
- **panic** (zone nothing beyond the seed city and buy units while threatened or outnumbered) as a
  lower-tier handicap: a Hard helper with it won 12-17 of 80 against Hard enemies where it won 24
  (`hs-panic-*`), but a Medium helper with it could not beat Easy enemies on missions 7, 8 and 10
  at any strength (`grid-p8631`, `grid-p12841`); removed;
- Army-first openings and holding zoning until the army was safe (-8 to -15 and -2 of 80); removed;
- longer goals with later first attacks (`goals-v1`: Brutal v Hard 58/96, Hard v Hard 56/96) and the
  existing combat switches (focus fire, turret and repair knock-outs; best +8 of 80).

### Enemy strength per mission

`[DIFFICULTY]` sets each tier's enemy strength per mission:

| Mission | Grace | Easy | Medium | Hard | Brutal | First wave, minute (Easy / Medium / Hard / Brutal) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Sihaya Basin | 10 | 6.8 | 5 | 3.25 | 3.35 | 1.5 / 2 / 3 / 3 |
| 2 Hagal Flats | 9 | 4.3 | 2.95 | 2.95 | 3.25 | 2 / 3 / 3 / 3 |
| 3 Ash Quarter | 18 | 6 | 3.3 | 2.85 | 2.95 | 3 / 5 / 6 / 6 |
| 4 Arrakeen Blackout | 45 | 8.4 | 3.6 | 3.2 | 3.35 | 5 / 12 / 14 / 13 |
| 5 Cielago Watch | 3 | 1.2 | 1.35 | 1.55 | 1.6 | 2 / 2 / 2 / 2 |
| 6 Harg Pass Convoy | 3 | 0.85 | 0.95 | 1 | 1.1 | 4 / 3 / 3 / 3 |
| 7 Shield Wall Breach | 40 | 1.55 | 1.3 | 1.7 | 1.65 | 26 / 31 / 24 / 24 |
| 8 Tuono Crossing | 13 | 2.15 | 0.9 | 0.95 | 1.05 | 6 / 14 / 14 / 12 |
| 9 Carthag Vise | 10 | 2.55 | 2.3 | 2.4 | 2.5 | 4 / 4 / 4 / 4 |
| 10 All Against Atreides | 5 | 1.45 | 1 | 1 | 1.45 | 3 / 5 / 5 / 3 |

"Grace" is the authored `FirstEnemyAttack`; the first wave comes at grace / strength.

The values come from a strength grid: every mission at x0.4, 0.6, 0.85, 1, 1.2, 1.7 and 2.4 of a
base value, all six pairings, eight seeds (`grid-v1`, `grid-v1b`, `grid-m37`, `grid-m47`), pooled
with two fresh-seed validations at the values they played (`val-r1`, `val-final`): 24 seeds per
mission near the chosen values. A logistic fit per pairing (per-mission intercepts and slopes, the
slopes shrunk towards the pairing's slope) gives the strength where:

- Medium and Hard: the same-tier helper wins at most 35% and the helper one tier up at least 55%,
  aiming the one-tier-up rate at a target that falls from 85% on mission 1 to 55% on mission 10;
  where no strength satisfies both, the same-tier rule (at most 40%) wins;
- Easy: a Medium helper wins at that falling target;
- Brutal: a Brutal helper wins at most 35% falling to 25%.

The values need not rise from Easy to Brutal: a slower tier needs more starting credits to be a
match for the same helper (mission 1: Easy 6.8, Brutal 3.35). Easy enemies barely attack, so on
missions 3, 4, 8 and 10 a Medium helper still wins more often than the falling target at x2.4, the
top of the grid.

The grace before the first wave is divided by the enemy's strength, so a mission whose enemies are
strong attacks early. Where that sent the first wave before either helper tier could finish its
city, the tiers did not separate, and the grace was lengthened instead: Ash Quarter 9 -> 18 minutes,
Arrakeen Blackout 8 -> 45 (8 at 3.5 sent the first wave at minute 2; every helper was overrun at
minutes 10-13), Shield Wall Breach 10 -> 40 (30 at 1.4-1.7 sent it at minutes 18-21, before a
Brutal helper reached 50,000 people at 22-31). With grace 45 Arrakeen Blackout separates the tiers
(fit at the shipped values: Hard v Hard 32%, Brutal v Hard 58%).

Ash Quarter and Shield Wall Breach still do not: at every strength a Brutal helper beats Hard
enemies about as often as a Hard helper does (fit at the shipped values: Hard v Hard 43% and
Brutal v Hard 39% on Ash Quarter, 42% and 44% on Shield Wall Breach). Their Hard values aim at the
same-tier rule, so a Brutal helper wins only about 40% there. On Shield Wall Breach a Brutal helper
reaches 50,000 people sooner (winning games: median minute 27 against 32 for a Hard helper,
`grid-m47` at x0.85-1.2) but loses as often, at minutes 27-47 after the breach; the cause is not
traced yet.

## Playtests

```sh
agent-workspace run --project dunecity --owner OWNER --tests --wait -- dunecity-queue submit \
  --purpose research --machines aws aws-mel2 --cwd /Users/stefan/Documents/projects/dunecity-feature-batch \
  --rev FULL_SHA --tag TAG -- python3 tests/ai/scenario-matrix.py --tier full --categories citycampaign \
  --results RESULTS
python3 tests/ai/city-campaign-report.py RESULTS
```

`--categories citycampaign` plays every mission with an idle human and a QuantBot helper against
enemy QuantBots in six pairings: one tier above the enemies (Brutal v Hard, Hard v Medium, Medium v
Easy) and the same tier (Brutal v Brutal, Hard v Hard, Medium v Medium), four seeds each by
default (`--seeds-per-cell`, `--city-missions`, `--campaign-seeds`), 120 game minutes (rounds up to
v9 used 90). `win_type` records
`objectives`, `survival` or `conquest`.

To recalibrate the strengths, play a grid (`--city-strength 0.4 0.6 0.85 1 1.2 1.7 2.4
--campaign-seeds` eight seeds, about an hour for ten missions on the two workers), then fit:

```sh
python3 tests/ai/city-campaign-calibrate.py GRID [VALIDATION...] --eval   # predictions and fitted values
python3 tests/ai/city-campaign-calibrate.py GRID [VALIDATION...] --write  # into the generator
python3 scripts/gen_city_campaign.py
```

The script takes each game's strength from its `-sS` multiplier and the generator at the game's
source commit, leaves out games whose mission had another grace period then, and searches only
the strengths the games played. Validate the new values on fresh seeds: with eight seeds a single
mission's rate moves by about 17 points from seed luck alone, so judge the six pairings over all
ten missions (80 games each) and read single missions from the fit.

Lessons, each now fixed in the maps or the AI:

- Two Custom-rule enemies that each start richer than the player rush it in the first 5-13 minutes
  even against a Brutal helper; `FirstEnemyAttack` grace periods and player-favoured treasuries
  open the early missions, and both shrink towards mission 10.
- Pre-placed lots develop within a minute, so a prebuilt city's population is effectively its
  starting population: population goals sit well above it (Ash Quarter starts above 45,000).
- A treasury goal needs saving discipline: counting starting cash as progress froze Tuono's helper
  (14,000 of 15,000 at minute 0); spending freely until halfway left Brutal and Hard helpers on a
  few thousand credits; the earned-income floor fills Hagal by minute 11-14.
- Medium helpers beat Easy enemies on almost every mission in every round: Easy QuantBots barely
  attack. The curve is set by the Brutal v Hard and Hard v Medium pairings.
- With the same seeds and maps the games are deterministic: missions 1-6 of v8 reproduced v7 game
  for game, so a round only needs to replay the missions that changed.
- Shield Wall Breach is decided by the race between the basin city reaching its population goal and
  the assault that follows the breach (v9: lost games collapsed at 27-30 minutes).

That round's validation (eight seeds, 120 game minutes) had every one-tier-up pairing win every
mission most of the time, Brutal v Hard falling from 100% on missions 1-6 to 63% on mission 10. It
was superseded by the same-tier rule, the low-spice maps and the skill ladder.
